# Rules for facts, claims and voice (the gate and the reviewer enforce them)

- No number outside a fact record. A number that changes, or that sources disagree on (parameters, tokens, compute, scores, dates of releases, prices, sizes), is never plain text in the front matter or the prose. Write it as `{{fact:<item>/<field>}}`, and only when the fact record exists. Do not add a fact record: facts come from harvest. A past result of a fixed event, with its source, is a historical claim and is allowed.
- No "state of the art", "SOTA", "currently", "the best", "the largest", "the most capable", "leading", and no rank or superlative of a model, a lab, a benchmark score or a dataset, in prose or in the front matter. No word that rots: "recent", "recently", "new", "latest", "modern", "today", "nowadays", "now", "so far", "to date". A History section may say "at the time" about a past date that the sentence names.
- No position on a contested question in the site voice. When the field disagrees, give each serious position in the same form, with its holders and sources, and do not say which one is right. No vague hedge such as "some believe" or "many experts": a view has a named holder and a source, or it is not on the page.
- Attribute what a developer reports about its own model ("the developer reports"). Use "shows" and "proves" only for a proof. For empirical work use "reports", "finds", "measures", "observes" or "argues".
- A prediction is always attributed and dated, never stated in the site voice.
- No private fact about a living person: public roles and public work only.
- No status, level, badge or rank written by hand. The build computes them.
- Every claim sentence ends with its anchor `{{#c-<id>}}`, and `claims.yaml` has its record with a type, the scope of an empirical claim, and evidence with a locator.

# Style rules (the reviewer enforces them)

- Plain English in the style of ASD-STE100: short sentences (about 25 words at most), active voice, common words, one idea per sentence. Write in your own words. Never copy text, code or figures from a book, a paper or a web site.
- In the Markdown source, put each sentence on its own line. A paragraph is a group of lines with no blank line between them.
- No tab character, no em dash and no en dash anywhere. Write ranges as "2012 to 2017" or "0.1 to 0.5". Eponyms use a hyphen: "Kullback-Leibler".
- No filler or hype words. The list is `registers/banned-words.yaml` (spec 6.2, rule S03), and it includes "simple", "easy", "just", "clearly", "obviously", "of course" and "note that".
- Expand each acronym on first use on the page, with the acronym in parentheses.
- Do not describe a model as if it were a person (spec 6.1, principle 8).
- Math in KaTeX with the notation of `{spec}/06-content-standard.md` section 6.4: inline `$...$`, display `$$ ... $$` with an anchor such as `{{#eq-name}}`.
- Links: `[[slug]]` or `[[slug|text]]` to a page of this site, `[[al:slug]]`, `[[m2:slug]]` or `[[pg:slug]]` to a neighbour through its bridge card. Link a term the first time the page uses it. Only link to slugs that exist: check each slug with `grep -c '^<slug>\t' {slugs_file}` (the file lists every node, card and bridge card, one per line: slug, kind, area, name; bridge cards are `al:slug`). Never invent a link target. The same holds for every slug in the front matter.
- "In plain words": at most 150 words, no formula longer than one term, no symbol that the prerequisites do not define.
- History: who, when, where first published. Only state facts you are sure of and that a source in the list supports. If you are not sure of a year or a person, leave it out. Never guess.
- No placeholder, no TODO, no "left as an exercise" and no "it is easy to see". Every step of a derivation or a proof says why it holds.
